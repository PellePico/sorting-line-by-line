Five classic sorting algorithms in Python, run one line at a time. The highlighted line is the one that just ran.

SortThe idea in one lineWhat is final after one round?Moves items by…SpeedStops early if already sorted?
bubbleSwap neighbours that are in the wrong order, again and againThe last spot (biggest number)Many small swaps of neighboursO(n²)Yes → O(n)
selectionFind the smallest of the rest, swap it to the frontThe first spot (smallest number)One long swap per roundO(n²) alwaysNo
insertionTake the next card, slide it left into its placeNothing is final yet. The left part is only sorted among itselfSliding bigger cards rightO(n²)Yes → O(n)
mergeSplit in half until pieces of 1, then glue sorted piles togetherEach glued piece is sortedBuilding new lists (merge)O(n log n) alwaysNo, but it's fast anyway
quickPick a pivot, split into smaller / equal / bigger, repeat on each sideThe pivot (all equal ones) is in its final placeBuilding new lists (less / equal / greater)O(n log n) usually, O(n²) worstNo

The two pairs people mix up: Bubble vs selection: bubble fixes the end with lots of small neighbour swaps. Selection fixes the front with one swap per round. Merge vs quick: merge splits without thinking (just the middle) and does the hard work afterwards when gluing. Quick does the hard work first (splitting around the pivot), so gluing afterwards is just +.
